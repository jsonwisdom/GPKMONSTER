# Visual Contract — BOX_D_GPK_RENDER_GUARD_V1

This file defines the image-level contract used by Box D. It evaluates the rendered artifact, not merely the prompt.

## 1. Diegetic mechanism required

The institutional or legal mechanism must exist inside the scene as an object, force, costume, machine, environment, or physical action.

Examples of valid translations:

- burden of proof -> crushing weight/anvil/load
- discovery -> cabinet/flood/mouth consuming documents
- stay -> tape, lock, brace, or gavel physically preventing movement
- fee shifting -> money/pockets physically transferring sides
- public concern -> airborne question/megaphone/crowd pressure
- actual malice -> knowingly false publication embodied as a deliberate physical act

A label by itself does not satisfy this clause.

**Hard rule:** If the mechanism can be deleted and the remaining image still functions as generic gross-out art, verdict = DELTA.

## 2. Cause -> mechanism -> gross-out

The scene should read as a compact causal chain:

`CAUSE -> INSTITUTIONAL MECHANISM -> BODY/ENVIRONMENT CONSEQUENCE`

Grossness is the consequence or translation of the mechanism, not decoration.

## 3. One dominant victim and one predicament

Use one principal character or clearly dominant victim. The viewer should know who is paying the price and why. Crowds, secondary figures, and props may support the joke but cannot dissolve the focal predicament.

## 4. Name/title does conceptual work

The top title must carry the thesis. Prefer a short pun, clipped doctrine, mishearing, rhyme, or double meaning. The title should still be funny or conceptually useful without reading every prop.

## 5. Props act like verbs

Major props must act: crush, flood, bite, punch, tape, drain, burn, bury, split, explode, drag, trap, inflate, or otherwise perform the mechanism.

Automatic DELTA: floating citation or doctrine text that serves only as wallpaper.

## 6. Physical sticker-card language

Target an **original retro gross-out satirical trading card** with:

- distressed paper/sticker stock
- aged print texture and imperfect registration
- hand-inked/caricature energy
- strong top title
- single-scene framing
- compact edition/price/series ephemera when useful
- lower caption zone with a second punchline

Do not instruct an image model to imitate `Garbage Pail Kids`, `Topps`, a living artist, or a specific proprietary card design. The standard protects the satire mechanism, not third-party trade dress.

## 7. Caption is a ruling

The bottom caption must do more than repeat the title. It should make a deadpan ruling on the predicament in playground/sticker-card English.

Examples of the structural pattern:

- allocation: who carries it?
- procedure: what happens next?
- intent: what did the character know/do?
- consequence: who pays, gets stuck, gets buried, or gets shocked?

## 8. Legibility and text integrity

- title readable at card scale
- bottom caption readable
- requested labels spelled correctly
- no meaningless pseudo-text
- real legal citations accurate when used as real
- invented citations clearly fictionalized or omitted

Any unreadable critical text = DELTA.

## One-card-one-doctrine rule

One card is not a brief. Keep one primary legal or institutional mechanism per artifact. Related sister doctrines become separate objects unless the card's single mechanism genuinely requires them.

Mixing unrelated jurisdictions or statutes into one visual merely to look technical = DELTA or HOLD, depending on whether the render already exists.

## Automatic DELTA list

- generic political poster
- generic comic-book poster
- photoreal person plus slime
- random paper/data collage
- title treated like clean app typography
- generic AI smile when expression should carry the joke
- meaningless floating labels
- citation with no scene function
- missing central predicament
- missing title thesis
- missing lower punchline
- style with no institutional mechanism
- three benchmark cards remixed into one collage
- doctrine smoothie across unrelated statutes/jurisdictions
